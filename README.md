# FS25_WinchTow

The winch rope can be attached to the **attacher joints of other vehicles**, not only to trees and logs: pull a stuck tractor out, drag a trailer by its drawbar eye, move a vehicle a few metres without driving it. It works with every winch in the game (also other mods' winches): nothing in a winch's XML or model needs to change.

## Use
Stand at the winch, start the attach mode as usual, and look at an attacher joint of a vehicle (a tractor's rear or front hitch, a trailer's drawbar eye, an implement's link point): the **green ring** shows the joint, the attach key ties the rope to it. The rope length and the number of things a rope holds apply; the tree mass limit (`maxTreeMass`) does not, for a vehicle joint (the game's own check is overwritten for those). Any number of winches may hold one piece. Single player only. `wtProbe` (console) lists the attacher joints in front of you and how far each is from your view ray, which says why a joint is or is not picked.

## How it works (read from the game's scripts, 28 Oct 2026 archive)
Vanilla only ever targets trees: in attach mode `Winch:onPostUpdate` casts the player's view ray with `CollisionFlag.TREE`, `Winch:onWinchTreeRaycastCallback` accepts shapes with `getSplitType() ~= 0` and stores the target in `spec.treeRaycast` (`lastValidTree`, `treeTargetPos`, `treeCenterPos`, `treeUp`, `treeRadius`), the target marker (the green ring, `TargetTreeMarker`) and the attach prompt are driven from that, and `attachTreeToWinch` hooks onto the tree (`ForestryHook:mountToTree`) and makes the engine's physics rope between the winch and the tree's rigid body. This mod, in the same shape:

1. **Detect:** while a rope is in attach mode and no tree is targeted, the attacher joint (`spec_attacherJoints.attacherJoints[].jointTransform`, `spec_attachable.inputAttacherJoints[].node`) nearest to the view ray, within 0.35 m, of a vehicle in *another group* than the winch's, within the rope's reach and 5 m of the player, is injected as the valid target: `lastValidTree` is the vehicle's component node (a rigid body, so `getMass` and the physics rope work on it). Vanilla then draws the ring, makes the rope green and enables the prompt. While a joint is picked the tree raycast is held back, because its callback would reset the target every frame; looking away lets go.
2. **Attach:** `ForestryHook:mountToTree` is wrapped: for one of our targets the hook is linked to the joint node (a tree would get a belt). Everything else in `attachTreeToWinch` (the physics rope, the winch's joint limits, sounds, the mass limit) is vanilla.
3. **Save:** vehicle attachments cannot be restored from a split shape id, so `Winch:saveToXMLFile` leaves them out: after a reload the rope is not attached.

## The hand winch (a hand tool, no winch vehicle needed)

You want to nudge a heavy thing 30 cm because somebody has to get past, and fetching a tractor with a winch on it is silly. So: the **Hand winch** (shop, hand tools, miscellaneous, 150) hooks onto the attacher joint you look at (the same joints the winch rope can take, within 6 m): the tool's action key hooks, the same key lets go. A hooked vehicle creeps towards YOU at up to **2 km/h** while you stand more than 1.2 m from its joint, and stops when you stand near it: walk to where you want it. 30 cm is a hook, one step back, one step forward.

How it pulls: a force on the joint's body towards you, applied at the joint (a speed controller: force = mass x acceleration, at most 3 m/s2, no more once it moves at 2 km/h), so a pulled trailer swings round on its own wheels. A vehicle nobody sits in has its brake pedal down (the first test: a 12 t trailer did not move under 22 kN, the log said so), so the hand winch lets the brake off (`vehicle:brake(0)`: it also takes the standstill auto-hold with it) while it pulls, and puts it back when you stand near it or let go, so the vehicle stops where it is. Pulling for 2 s without moving is still written to the log once, with the mass and the force (`[WinchHand]`). The rope lets go when the tool is put away, at 15 m, or when the vehicle is gone. The rope is the game's own steel cable (`ForestryRope`, what the winch draws) from your hand to the joint, straight and shader-stretched every frame; a green debug line only when the engine pieces are missing, or with `WinchHand.ROPE = false`. The tool has no model yet (as the leash has none), and the shop image is the leash's, a placeholder.

**A trailer's tongue is carried, not pulled (`WinchHand.CARRY`).** The green line was always a placeholder, and the stop and go at 1.2 m was jerky when you stood about near the wagon (the brake went on and off right there). So a joint on a small body of its own (a tongue: 60 kg of a 660 kg wagon, not a machine's own hitch, not a joint on the main body) is hooked the way a horse's hitch does it: the support leg goes up (and the hook waits for the support animation, which on some trailers is the tongue's hinge), the tongue is lifted on its hinge to the height of a tractor's attacher joint (0.81 m, `EYE_HEIGHT_M`) with the wagon held where it is (brake on, no horizontal speed, so nothing rolls off a slope), then the eye is CARRIED: it trails you 0.8 m behind and is pulled with a force towards the speed it should have, which is your own speed away from it (smoothed: the capsule moves in steps) plus 4 per second per metre it is further behind, at most the pull speed of the wagon (13 km/h for a little one). It is never pushed, and when you stop the same force brakes it (the speed wanted is none), so the brake stays off the whole time and nothing flaps. No line is drawn for it (`SHOW_LINE` brings it back). Anything else (a tractor, a joint on the main body) is held at the end of a **stiff 2 m pole** (`WinchHand.POLE`, `POLE_M`): the same controller, but it is pulled in when it is further than 2 m from you and PUSHED BACK when it is nearer, so a tractor never ends up in your face, at three times the carry's gain (`POLE_GAIN`: a steel rod, not a rubber band), the brake off the whole time, the machine not lifted, and the pole drawn as the steel cable 3 cm thick. A tractor does not steer, its axles drag sideways and it used to swing round at you, so the pole has a **steering nudge** (`NUDGE`): a couple, two opposite forces (no net push) at the joint and at the far end of its body, turns the body along the pole, at most `NUDGE_MS2` (1 m/s2) times its mass and in proportion to how far across the pole it lies (full at 0.5 rad). The horse's tow bar is the same idea. `POLE = false` is the old pull above (the 1.2 m stop, brake on and off) and `CARRY = false` is the way back for the tongues.

Open (to try in the game): whether the brake release is enough (a vehicle attached to something, or on a slope), whether it should pull towards a fixed anchor instead of towards you, a rope model.

## The support leg

A trailer standing unhitched rests on a support leg (a landing gear: the peg under the drawbar). The game pulls it up when the trailer is attached to a tractor, by playing the trailer's support animation backwards (`Attachable:onPostAttach`), and puts it down when it is detached. A rope is no attachment, so the leg stayed down and dragged. Now a vehicle held by a winch rope, or by the hand winch, has its leg pulled up (the same animation, backwards) and put down again when the rope lets go. A vehicle that is attached to another one is left alone. Vehicles without a support animation do nothing (`[WinchTow]` says in the log when a leg moves).

**Logs (`WinchHand.LOGS`).** Look at a felled tree or a log lying on the ground instead of a joint and the same key hooks it at the point you look at (a node of its own on the log carries the rope and the force, so it turns with the log): it is DRAGGED behind you on the rope, pulled in when it is further than 2.5 m (`LOG_REACH_M`), never pushed, with at most 6 m/s2 (`LOG_ACCEL_MS2`) times its mass, and as slowly as it is heavy (the same pull speed as a vehicle of that mass, which caps you too). A tree that still stands (a static body) is felled first; a log belted onto a trailer or carried (kinematic) is not taken, and one that gets belted, cut or deleted while hooked is let go. The game's winch does it with a physics rope and a tension belt round the trunk; this is the hand version of the same idea, a force on the log's own rigid body. A joint in view always wins over a log.

A hooked log wears the game's own strap and hook (`ForestryHook`, `WinchHand.HOOK`) round the trunk where you looked, turned towards your hand, and the rope ends at the hook, as on the real winch. If the engine pieces are missing or it fails, the rope ends at the grip point instead. The ring before hooking is drawn round the trunk (centre, axis and radius from `SplitShapeUtil.getTreeOffsetPosition`).

**A wagon to a tractor.** Hook a wagon's drawbar eye with the hand winch (or the horse mod's leash), then look at a free hitch of a tractor: when the hitch takes the eye's type (the game attaches only joints of the same type) the green ring is on it, and the click attaches the wagon with the game's own attach (the call the Q key makes), as long as the eye is within 3 m of the hitch (`WinchHand.ATTACH_REACH_M`: walk it over first, it follows you). A hitch of another type, one with something on it, or a refusal by the game is said in words and the wagon stays hooked.

### Hitching and unhitching with the hand winch

* **Hitch:** hook an eye or a hitch (it does not matter which of the two vehicles you pull), bring it within 3 m of the other vehicle's fitting joint and click. The fitting joint (a hitch for an eye, an eye for a hitch, the same type, free) is chosen for you: the one nearest to the hooked joint, so nothing has to be aimed at even when the joints sit a few centimetres apart. A ring shows the one it will take. Further away the click says how far. **Bridge pieces are the exception:** they are wired to the piece you AIM at (any joint of it within 2.5 m of the view ray, so aim at its body), never to whichever one lies nearest; aiming at none, the click just lets go. And a piece that lies the other way round (a joint of the wrong kind of it is more than 0.5 m nearer than the fitting one) is refused with "turn it around", not wired across its whole length to the far end.
* **Wires between bridge pieces (`scripts/WinchWire.lua`).** Two pieces that carry the Hammer's bridge function are not hitched by a click. Hook a joint and click the fitting joint of the other piece (up to 25 m away): a steel wire is laid between the two, nothing moves. Do the same for the second pair. Then **hold the left mouse button** (the tool's ordinary button; held 0.3 s with nothing hooked it reels, a shorter press is the usual click that hooks the next joint) and ALL wires are reeled in at once (the game's `WINCH_CONTROL` key, K, does the same where it works): every loose piece is pulled along its wire(s) with 6 m/s2 times its mass (softer in the last 40 cm), at most 0.8 m/s, with 35 % of its weight lifted so the ground does not hold it; a pinned or frozen piece stays. When every wire between the two pieces is shorter than 6 cm they are joined: the first wire with the game's own attach, the second one with the Hammer's locked joint. Wires of different length are reeled unevenly: each wire pulls with its length over the longest one's (at least 15 %, `MIN_SHARE`), so the pieces swing in and all wires run out together. **A chain hanging on the piece** (grates hitched to it) counts: the pull is 6 m/s2 times the whole chain's mass (`getTotalMass`), at most 12 times the piece's own (`CHAIN_CAP`); the lift stays that of the piece itself. Ctrl and a click (or the unhitch key) on a piece takes its wires away. `WinchWire.ACCEL_MS2`, `LIFT_SHARE`, `MAX_SPEED_MS`, `DONE_M` are the knobs.
* **Frozen or pinned:** a vehicle with wheels held by the Hammer is refused (the pull would take its axle off the frame, the ring is blue). One without wheels (a bridge grate) can be hooked and hitched. Two hitched bridge pieces take a second pair: hook a free hitch, click, and the Hammer's BridgeLink swings the loose piece in and joins it.
* **Unhitch:** Left Shift and a click on a joint that is hitched (the one you look at, else the hooked one): an eye is let go from what pulls it, a hitch lets go of what hangs on it (the game's own detach), and the hook is let go.
* **No wheels:** a vehicle without wheels (a plate, a bridge grate) is dragged like a log: its pull as a vehicle lets the wheels roll it, on the ground it only rubs.
* **Ctrl and a click** does the same as the unhitch key, on the hooked vehicle, else on the vehicle whose BODY you look at (a bridge panel's middle: that panel comes loose from both neighbours, all four joints, wires and second joints included, and nothing else is touched), else on the one whose joint you look at.
* **Unhitch from everything:** the key **U** (rebindable, "Winch: unhitch from everything") with the hand winch in the hand: the vehicle that is hooked, or whose joint you look at, is unhitched from what pulls it and from all that hangs on it, and the hook is let go.

**Cable collision (`scripts/WinchCable.lua`):** a wire laid with the hand winch gets collision once both its ends have been still for a second: one body of 1 m boxes (5 cm square, `assets/cableSegment.i3d`, made by `tools/make_cable_segment.py`), group STATIC_OBJECT, mask PLAYER only, so a person can stand on it and walk it while the vehicles it is tied to never touch it. An end that moves (reeling, a push) removes it at once; it is built again after the wait. The wires are not saved yet, so neither is the collision. `WinchCable.ENABLED` switches it off; the log says `[WinchCable] cable built / removed`.

**A wire between two latches (the poles):** with the hand winch look at a winch latch and click: on a loose piece it is hooked as before (it follows you); on a PINNED or frozen one (a pole on its bank) nothing is pulled, the rope just waits (up to `LATCH_ROPE_MAX_M` 150 m). Walk to another latch (of another vehicle, up to `WinchWire.LATCH_MAX_M` 120 m), look at it (the green ring shows) and click: a wire is laid between the two. It joins nothing (`wire.latch`), the winch keys reel it like any wire (a loose piece is pulled towards the other end), and once both ends have been still for a second it gets collision (see WinchCable). Like every wire it is not saved yet.

**Swing damping (`WinchWire.SWING_DAMP`):** two half chains hanging from two banks swing against each other like pendulums and never meet (every pair must be under 25 cm at the same moment). While a wire is being reeled, or has been reeled in, the sideways speed of its piece (across the wire) is damped (6 m/s2 per m/s, at most 25 m/s2, shared between the wires of a piece) and its spin dies down (3 per second); speed along the wire is left to the reel. Latch wires between poles are not damped. **A second wire by itself:** the first wire between two bridge pieces also lays the wire for the second pair (the other hitch and the other eye that lie closest together) at once, so one click does both and the left button only has to reel. **For the Hammer's guide:** `WinchHand.layLatchWire(a, b)` and `WinchHand.hasWireBetween(a, b)` (on `g_currentMission.winchTow`).

**The engine takes at most 40 children per compound body** (`Failed to create compound cableRoot with 69 children. Maximum is 40`): a cable longer than 32 m got no collision at all (found 2026-11-17, the short ones worked). The engine cannot stretch a collision shape, so a cable is a row of fixed boxes: they were 1 m (69 for 68 m), now 4 m (17 for 68 m, one body); a body is still capped at `WinchCable.CHUNK` 32 boxes.

**Latch wires are saved (`winchWires.xml`):** a wire between two latches (poles, anchors) is written to the savegame folder whenever the game saves (the two vehicles' unique ids and their joint numbers), and laid again at a load as soon as both vehicles exist (for a minute; `SAVEGAME_LOADED` starts it, `WinchWire.layPending` runs it every update), unless that pair is wired already (the Hammer's guide lays the pole wires by itself). Wires between bridge pieces (hitch and eye) are the reeling of a moment and are not saved. The cable collision comes back with them once the ends have been still for a second.
